# Public Provident Fund (PPF)

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a long-term savings scheme introduced by the Government of India, backed by the central government. It is one of the most popular tax-saving instruments under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act.

## Key Features
- **Tenure:** 15 years (extendable by 5 years at a time)
- **Interest Rate:** Set quarterly by the government (currently ~7.1% p.a., tax-free)
- **Minimum Deposit:** ₹500 per financial year
- **Maximum Deposit:** ₹1.5 lakh per financial year
- **Deposits:** Minimum 1 deposit per year for 15 years

## Tax Benefits
- Investment up to ₹1.5 lakh is deductible under Section 80C
- Interest earned is tax-free (exempt-exempt-exempt status)
- Maturity proceeds are completely tax-free
- Partial withdrawals allowed from year 7 onwards

## Eligibility
- Any Indian resident individual can open a PPF account
- Minors can have accounts operated by guardians
- NRIs cannot open new accounts but can continue existing ones until maturity
- HUFs are no longer eligible (since Budget 2019)

## Loan Facility
- Loans available from year 3 to year 6
- Maximum loan: 25% of balance at end of year 2
- Interest rate: 1% above prevailing PPF rate
- Second loan allowed before full repayment of first

## Partial Withdrawal
- Allowed once per year from year 7 onwards
- Maximum: 50% of balance at end of year 6 (or preceding year)
- Only one withdrawal permitted per financial year

## PPF vs EPF vs VPF
- PPF: Individual account, max ₹1.5L/yr, 15-yr lock-in, sovereign guarantee
- EPF: Employer-linked, max limit varies, available on resignation
- VPF: Voluntary contribution to EPF beyond mandatory 12%

## Tips
- Best for risk-averse investors seeking tax-free returns
- Combine with ELSS for better post-tax returns if willing to take equity risk
- Extension after 15 years earns same interest rate with unlimited deposits
- Nomination facility available
